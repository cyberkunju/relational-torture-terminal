WHY THIS EXISTS

Most brain games train the wrong thing.

The usual stuff

Memory games and N-Back drills mostly grow how much you can hold in your head. Useful, but it plateaus fast and rarely transfers to real thinking.

What we train instead

The ability to derive relationships you were never told. Psychologists call this Relational Frame Theory, and it sits underneath reading, math, debugging, and reasoning itself.

Why it pays off

Relational skill is one of the few things shown to nudge fluid intelligence — your on-the-spot problem solving — rather than just rehearsing one narrow task.

03

OPTIONAL DIFFICULTY MODIFIERS

CRANK UP THE CHAOS

Leave these off for a clean start. Switch them on once the base game feels easy — that's when the real gains begin.

GIBBERISH MODE

Real names turn into nonsense tokens (Flib-10, Zoop-24). Strips away familiarity so you reason on structure alone.

STROOP MODE

Words get painted in colors that fight their meaning. Your brain must ignore the visual noise and trust the text.

NEGATION MODE

Clues hide behind double negatives — "is not slower than" instead of "is faster than". Untangle before you answer.
